## Details to preserve

- CPU job limits use the kernel's default termination action. That action
  does not send `END_OF_JOB_TIME` unless the alternative notification-only
  action is selected. The final job accounting identifies CPU exhaustion;
  do not enable the notification-only action and inadvertently remove the
  kernel limit. Enforcement is periodically checked, not a precise wall timer.
- A deadline abandons a wait, not ownership of a child. `proc.run` retains
  its otherwise-hidden child until completion even after collection. HTTP
  cancellation retains request memory until its completion, discards partial
  downloads, and leaves existing destinations intact. Tests cover both.
- ConPTY's child must be started with `STARTF_USESTDHANDLES` and all three
  standard handles set to `INVALID_HANDLE_VALUE` while inheriting no handles.
  Leaving these implicit attached the child to ConPTY but let its banner and
  ordinary reads/writes use the parent's redirected handles. The regression
  uses actual `ReadConsoleW`, Unicode input, and a redirected outer process.
- The console uses `ReleasePseudoConsole` after launch and ultimately
  `ClosePseudoConsole`. Our named-pipe ends are overlapped; the console ends
  are synchronous. Tests cover descendant/conhost cleanup, immediate closes,
  a parked reader, large output, prompt rewrites, empty patterns, partial VT
  and UTF-8, deadlines, and persistent overflow errors.
- ASAN owns the unhandled-exception hook. Its intentional `--crash-test`
  assertion checks the sanitizer report; production still checks kuu's
  normal exit-3 crash report. Process-name tests derive the runtime basename.
